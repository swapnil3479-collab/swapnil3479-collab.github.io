*This is book five of the Throne of Glass series. Nothing below reveals what happens in any book, but the review assumes you've read the first four. If you haven't, start with [Throne of Glass](throne-of-glass.html).*

## Summary (spoiler-free)

With war approaching, the heroine and the people around her are forced to take sides, gather allies and decide what they are willing to risk. Old loyalties are tested, friendships break and are mended, and the magic that has been building throughout the series becomes harder and harder to ignore.

As in the previous volumes, the story follows several viewpoints at once. The book is about building up strength before a coming conflict, and it ends in a way that makes the next book feel unavoidable.

## Review

*Empire of Storms* is the biggest book in the series so far, in scope and in length. At roughly 690 pages it handles a large cast across many storylines, and when it works, it works well. The strongest arcs are those of secondary characters, with a couple of them being given the best material in the book. The emotional beats land, the world feels more connected, and the last chapters hit hard, with an ending that will leave most readers desperate for the next volume. Fans of the ensemble approach will find a lot to enjoy.

The weaknesses are also clear. Romance takes up a large share of the page count, and some readers feel it comes at the expense of the plot. The middle section is slow, and several chapters feel more like padding than progress. A few characters behave in ways that feel inconsistent with how they were drawn earlier, and a handful of voices start to sound alike. Some readers also miss characters who are absent for most of the book. There is also a feeling of repeated patterns in how the author builds relationships, which will be more noticeable if you've read her other series.

On Goodreads it averages about 4.63 out of 5 from over 1.4 million ratings, slightly above the last book. Independent reviewers I read were mostly warm, with one praising the character arcs while noting that the middle drags. That balance seems fair: it is enjoyable and ambitious, but it asks for patience.

## Who should read it

Readers who have enjoyed the ensemble format and want a large, emotional buildup to the finale. If the heavy romance or the long middle were a problem for you in earlier books, they will be again here.

## Verdict

A sprawling, emotional and gripping chapter with strong secondary arcs, weighed down by a sagging middle and a heavy focus on romance.

**Rating: 4 out of 5**
