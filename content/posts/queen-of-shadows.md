*This is book four of the Throne of Glass series. Nothing below reveals what happens in any book, but the review assumes you've read the first three. If you haven't, start with [Throne of Glass](throne-of-glass.html).*

## Summary (spoiler-free)

Book three spread its characters across several storylines and kept them apart. *Queen of Shadows* is where those separate paths begin to pull together. The heroine arrives with a clear purpose, unfinished business and a plan, and the other threads, which looked unrelated for so long, start to collide in the same places.

The book is a large, plot-heavy installment. It balances a political and military struggle against a very personal one, and it is built to pay off ideas the series has been setting up since the first volume.

## Review

This is the book where the series' long game starts to land. Maas has spent three volumes laying down plotlines and character arcs, and here many of them meet. The result is a more confident and more satisfying story than anything before it, and the ensemble cast is the best argument for that. Several of the women in the story get real room to grow, and their different strengths and flaws are the engine of the book. The action scenes are tighter than in earlier volumes, the stakes feel higher, and the last stretch is intense and fast enough that it is hard to stop reading.

It does have problems. At about 650 pages it is long, and a sizable share of the first half is setup. Readers who like a quick start will feel the wait before things speed up. Some character relationships are handled in ways that feel inconsistent with how they were written earlier, and some romantic developments feel sudden. Reviewers I read describe the same pattern: a slow opening followed by a much stronger second half. Heavy attention on a few popular characters can also crowd out other parts of the story.

Goodreads readers give it about 4.62 out of 5 from over 1.6 million ratings, and it won the Goodreads Choice Award for Young Adult Fantasy in 2015. Many fans call it the best in the series. I agree that it is a high point, though the pacing and some character choices keep it just short of a perfect score.

## Who should read it

Anyone who made it through book three. This is the payoff many readers were waiting for, and it works much better if you've been patient with the earlier books. Don't start here.

## Verdict

An ambitious, emotionally satisfying and often thrilling turning point that rewards loyal readers, held back slightly by a long runway and some uneven character handling.

**Rating: 4 out of 5**
