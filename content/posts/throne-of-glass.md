## Summary (spoiler-free)

Celaena Sardothien was once the most feared assassin in the kingdom of Adarlan. Now she is a prisoner, worn down by a year of hard labor in the salt mines of Endovier. Then the Crown Prince offers her a deal. She can enter a tournament against twenty-three of the realm's deadliest thieves, killers and soldiers, with the winner becoming the king's personal champion. After four years of service, the winner earns freedom.

Celaena has every reason to hate the king who rules from the glass castle. But the alternative is Endovier, so she takes the offer. She is trained by the captain of the guard, and the tournament runs alongside an unsettling mystery inside the castle walls. Magic has been banned across the land, and something dark seems to be stirring anyway. This is the first book in a long series, so it spends much of its time setting up a world and a cast.

## Review

The book's biggest strength is momentum. The premise (an assassin competing for her freedom) is an easy hook, and Maas keeps the chapters short and the plot moving. At about 430 pages it reads quickly, and many readers finish it in a couple of sittings. The castle setting is atmospheric, and the quieter threads are where the book is most interesting. The mystery of what is happening inside the palace is the strongest of them. The friendship between Celaena and one of the secondary characters is another, and is given more care than most of the romance. The book also plants plenty of seeds for later volumes, and the worldbuilding feels deliberately larger than what this first book shows.

The weaknesses are real, though, and Goodreads reviewers raise them often. The heroine is introduced as a deadly, hardened killer, but the book spends more time on her appearance, her wardrobe, her books and her romantic attention than on anything that makes the title believable. She is almost too talented and too attractive to be vulnerable. The tournament, which sells the premise, gets surprisingly little page time. Several of the competitors are thinly drawn, so the stakes of the trials rarely land as hard as they should. The prose leans on telling over showing, and some descriptions are overwrought. The romantic subplot is serviceable but familiar, and the two men involved are not distinct enough to make it compelling.

For context, the book averages about 4.18 out of 5 on Goodreads across more than 2.7 million ratings, so most readers enjoy it more than this review does. Much of that goodwill appears to come from the series as a whole, and later books are widely said to be stronger and darker. As a first volume, this one is more of a launch pad than a complete, polished story.

## Who should read it

Readers who like fast, plot-driven young adult fantasy with court intrigue and a slow-building romance will have a good time. It also suits anyone who wants an easy entry point into a long series. If you want a gritty, believable assassin story, or prose that is sharp and restrained, you may find it frustrating.

## Verdict

An entertaining and highly readable opening that is held back by an uneven lead character and a tournament that never quite delivers on its promise. It is a decent start rather than a standout, and its main value is the world and the questions it sets up for what follows.

**Rating: 3 out of 5**
