*This is book two of the Throne of Glass series. Nothing below reveals how book one ends, but it assumes you've read it. If you haven't, start with [our review of Throne of Glass](throne-of-glass.html).*

## Summary (spoiler-free)

Celaena Sardothien is back in the glass castle at Adarlan, but her position at court is not what it was. Her freedom is still tied to the king's will, and the price of it is paid in ways she finds harder to stomach than she expected. She is expected to do the king's dirty work, and she is surrounded by people who watch her closely and care about her, and who she cannot afford to trust completely.

As the political pressure builds, Celaena finds herself caught between loyalty, her own conscience and a growing sense that something much darker than one cruel king is at work in the kingdom. The book is a slower, more political story than the first, and it widens the cast and the stakes while keeping the castle at its center.

## Review

This is a clear step up from the first book, and most readers agree. Where *Throne of Glass* promised a lot and showed little, *Crown of Midnight* delivers on more of it. The story is darker, the plotting is tighter, and there are several moments of real emotional weight. Maas also does a better job of making the stakes feel personal. A few of the quieter relationships are the strongest parts of the book, and the second half moves at a pace that is hard to put down. The worldbuilding expands in satisfying ways, and the use of more than one point of view gives the cast more depth than the first book allowed.

It is not flawless, though. The first half is slow, and readers who loved the momentum of book one may find it testing their patience. Celaena's characterization is still uneven. Her sharp, dangerous side and her more indulgent side do not always sit comfortably together, and some personality shifts feel abrupt. The romance is better than before but still feels forced to some readers, and the lengthy attention to clothes and appearance remains a distraction. A few emotional beats are not quite earned by the setup, and the tone swings between light banter and heavy darkness more than it should.

The Goodreads average is about 4.36 out of 5 from more than two million ratings, and the praise is mostly about this jump in quality. I agree with the direction of that response more than its size. It is a good book that fixes many of its predecessor's problems, though not all of them.

## Who should read it

Anyone who finished book one and was curious enough to continue will likely be rewarded. If you found the first book too shallow, this one is where the series starts to earn its reputation. If the heroine, the romance or the prose bothered you the first time, expect improvement but not a transformation.

## Verdict

A more confident, darker and more emotionally engaging sequel that still stumbles on pacing and characterization. It is a solid reason to keep going with the series.

**Rating: 4 out of 5**
