*This is book six of the Throne of Glass series. Nothing below reveals what happens in any book, but the review assumes you've read the first five. If you haven't, start with [Throne of Glass](throne-of-glass.html).*

## Summary (spoiler-free)

*Tower of Dawn* does something unusual for a late-series installment: it steps away from the main storyline. The book is set on a different continent, at the same time as the events of [*Empire of Storms*](empire-of-storms.html), and follows characters who have been on the edge of the story until now. Its themes are recovery, trust and building alliances in an unfamiliar land, with a new culture, new politics and several new characters to learn.

Because it runs alongside the previous book, it isn't a direct continuation. The big questions from the last volume are not answered here. The book fills in a side of the world that the earlier volumes only touched, and it lays groundwork the finale will rely on.

## Review

What this book does best is give a bigger world a shape. The setting is rich in detail and culture, and the new characters bring a fresh perspective after five books of the same main viewpoints. The themes of healing and acceptance are treated with patience, and the story's handling of recovery and disability has been widely praised by readers. The slow-burn relationships are well built, and a few revelations help explain things that have puzzled readers across the series.

The biggest criticism is length and pace. At about 660 pages it is long for a side story, and the first half in particular moves slowly. Some chapters repeat the same emotional beats or descriptive habits, and one early stretch tests goodwill for the main character. Readers who came for the central cast and the cliffhanger from the last book may be frustrated to leave them behind. Others feel the book is more of a setup for the finale than a story that stands on its own. A reviewer I read called parts of it a chore, while others found it more rewarding than expected, and both reactions are fair.

Goodreads readers rate it about 4.28 out of 5 from more than 1.2 million ratings, the lowest score among the later books but still a high one. That seems about right: it is worthwhile, but it is the least essential-feeling volume in the series.

## When to read it

Several guides report that the publisher advises finishing *Empire of Storms* first, because this book takes place at the same time and reading it earlier would spoil parts of that story. The usual advice is to read it after *Empire of Storms* and before *Kingdom of Ash*, since the finale depends on what happens here. Some fans prefer a chapter-by-chapter tandem read, but those orders are made by readers rather than the author.

## Who should read it

Fans who want more world and more character depth, and who don't mind a slower pace. If you were only here for the main storyline, you will find it a detour, but it is a detour the ending relies on.

## Verdict

A thoughtful, well-built and emotionally rewarding side story that is too long and slow to match its neighbors. Worth reading before the finale, though not the series at its best.

**Rating: 3 out of 5**
